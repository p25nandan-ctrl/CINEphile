- The king is unnamed and no period is given; the link to Srirangapatna’s historical rulers is not made by the source.
- The repository image caption (Exotic India art) is a PDF artefact and not source material.
- Hindi spelling of Srirangapatnam and Nanjayya, and all Hindi, not yet checked by a fluent reader.
- Licence for the supplied text is unconfirmed.

### abo-tani-and-the-gift-of-agriculture

- Origin-myth and religious content (Mopin, Donyi-Polo context): have a Galo reader or community member review wording, names and tone before launch. The repository warns these stories are not representative of every community in Arunachal Pradesh.
- Mixed attribution: Abo Tani is shared across Adi, Galo, Nyishi and Apatani (Tani) communities with differing narratives; Mopin is specifically Galo. The text says it is an abridged retelling of a much longer myth, so details may differ from Galo tellings.
- Original article (Dr T. Padu, The Arunachal Times, 6 April 2022) could not be opened; verify author name and details. The web search showed an author byline of Dr Tejum Padu on that newspaper but this was not matched to the article.
- Sensitive content: sacrifice of mithuns, deception (the raft trick), polygamous marriage, and the dog eating the rice. All kept as supplied; check suitability for the age group.
- Spelling of names (Tode-Rene, Takar-Taji, Digo Pine, Kipu, Dire Kibo, Yapom, Yaji, Diyi Tami, Rosi Tami) and the Hindi renderings are approximate; check with a Galo speaker.
- The festival link in the story’s last paragraphs reads as the retelling’s framing; confirm how Galo people connect the myth to Mopin celebrations.
- Hindi translations are by an AI and need review by a fluent Hindi reader.
- The scene prompts about forest deities and Digo Pine are imagined; no traditional visual depiction was verified.
- Licence and permission status of the retelling are unknown.
- Level 2 averages about 14.4 words per sentence, the same as Level 3 (above the 9-12 target); an educator should consider shortening Level 2 sentences.

### bholi-the-cow-and-dubo-the-calf

### bihula-and-bishahari

- Source retelling: the repository gives no author or URL for the exact text; confirm which reference it follows and its licence.
- Sensitive content: the deaths of Chando's sons and of Bala (snakebite) are kept as in the source; check that the handling suits Level 1 readers (grades 4-5).
- Details vary between tellings: other sources (Folkartopedia) have five Bishahari sisters, a Vishwakarma-built iron house, Chando worshipping with his left hand, and no dance; the supplied text says the dance is only in some tellings.
- Name spellings differ across sources (Lakhindar/Lakhendra, Bishahari/Bisahari/Vishahari); Hindi spellings chosen by the AI need a native check.
- The supplied text says Bala was placed on a raft, while Sahapedia describes a covered manjusha box; the Manjusha-painting description is kept as supplied.
- Art details (colours, motifs) come from Sahapedia only; verify with an artist or another source before using in published illustrations.
- Hindi has not been reviewed by a fluent reader; Level 3 Hindi is deliberately formal.

- Source book Munda Lok Kathayein not verified; licence and permission for CCF text unconfirmed.

### dighal-thengia

- The supplied retelling differs from the NEZINE translation: no tail pulled off, no tigers on a mango tree, and no thief reform; and NEZINE’s footnote says Dighal Thengia means rain, which this version does not say. Only the supplied events were used.
- Source note says the retelling is ‘newly written’; the author and rights of the supplied text are unclear. Check permission before public use.
- The note about the 2021 oral episode narrated by Monisha Kalita was not verified.
- Hindi spelling of Dighal Thengia (दीघल ठेंगिया) and the Assamese pronunciation should be checked by a fluent reader; the tale’s Hindi translations and L1/L2 adaptations are unreviewed (AI-written).
- Art tradition: Sattriya manuscript painting is verified as an Assamese art but was used for religious and court texts; use here is only suggested inspiration.
